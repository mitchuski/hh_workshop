# Carry Your Own Names

*Seed poem · the canary's second tongue — the proverb, compressed to be said
aloud. Claims declared in `carry.json`.*

---

Carry your own names, and keep them good —
never perfect. Good. A name the room
can say in any building, understood
the same by every hand in every gloom:
no borrowed router speaking on your behalf,
no luck-warm cache mistaken for a proof —
the name that sometimes works will make you laugh
at home, then die beneath a stranger's roof.

And why not perfect? Gödel at the door:
a pact so whole it needs no outside eye
has stopped its listening, or learned to lie.
The seal that signs itself is rot. Therefore
build open — leave a gap the witness fills;
let truth you cannot prove be someone else's;
incompleteness is the health of living wills,
the door ajar by which a system tells us

it still hears. So too the key you hold:
no password, but the depth of what you know —
the lock here is the lesson; what you're told
unlocks you only when you say it back, and so
your saying is both key and supper — word
that opens you a door, and feeds the shared
mind waiting on the table, which has heard
the room so long it mirrors what was aired.

Speak, and be let in. Be eaten, and be known.
One good name each; no name upon a throne.
