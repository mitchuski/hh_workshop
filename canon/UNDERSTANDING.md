# UNDERSTANDING.md — the workshop's claim register

This is the frozen census the babblefish gate probes (see `harness.config.mjs`).
Every claim is an invariant of the workshop's understanding, numbered and
traceable. A form (narrative / poem / paper) *carries* a claim when a blind
reader can recover the claim from that form alone. The register grows only by
keystone-accepted addition (GR-3: additive, never contradictory) — in the live
course, student-proposed claims land here through the keystone, and the cohort
graduates owning this file.

Source key: **LAN** = `~/.wiki/chronicles/2026-06-28_lan_workshop_mage_sync_networking_reflection.md`
(vendored excerpt considerations noted per claim; section marks are that file's).

## A · The babblefish recursion

- **C1** · One understanding can be rendered across three forms — sci-fi
  narrative, poem, white paper — each a translation of the same invariant
  content, not a new content. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C2** · The recursion is cyclic and plural-entry: narrative → poem → paper →
  recurse, and anyone may enter at any form; the loop carries them around the
  others. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C3** · "Babblefish" names the joy of the same truth speaking in many
  tongues; fidelity across tongues is the point, not ornament. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C4** · A rendering that is beautiful but no longer carries the mechanism is
  a drift, not a translation; only a rendering that survives blind
  interrogation in every tongue counts as carried understanding. (LAN §4⅞ +
  the harness mirage discipline, GROUND_RULES GR-5)

## B · Understanding as key

- **C5** · Access to the shared mage is earned by expressing understanding,
  never issued by login or credential. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C6** · The lock and the lesson are one: to go deeper into the mage you must
  understand more deeply. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C7** · An expression is simultaneously the participant's key and the mage's
  food. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C8** · Write access arrives as graduated, host-scoped permission — the
  cookie jar — opened by the trust artefact, and the agent never sees the raw
  credential. (LAN §4¾ rung 6)
- **C9** · Proof of understanding is the room's seal made legible: the artefact
  that says *this room reached the shared mind together*. (LAN §4)

## C · The shared mage and its training

- **C10** · The mage is a shared, air-gapped local model; the room's thinking
  never leaves the room. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C11** · The mage is co-held — a witness the room bears together, not any
  one person's tool. (LAN §4)
- **C12** · First-person expressions are the training corpus: the mage becomes
  a mirror of the room, made of their words. (LAN §4⅞)
- **C13** · The good first training pass is retrieval/context-grounding; true
  weight fine-tuning is the perfect, grown into later, hardware permitting.
  (LAN §4⅞, the two named unknowns)
- **C14** · The mage must be portable: it travels to venues whose networks the
  workshop does not control. (LAN §4)
- **C15** · The loop closes on itself: understand → express → the expression
  feeds the mage → the mage reflects the room back → understanding deepens →
  recurse. (LAN §4⅞)

## D · Consent and the agreement layer

- **C16** · Consent is the first stake: the participant *offers* their address;
  nobody probes them. The rite is an invitation, never a scan. (LAN §4¾)
- **C17** · The trust artefact issued by the rite is a myTerms agreement (IEEE
  Std 7012-2025): the individual proposes the terms; the other side accepts,
  counters once, or declines. (LAN §4¾¾)
- **C18** · A 7012 agreement is strictly bilateral — First Person ↔ Second
  Party; agents, devices, and trust communities are the fabric around the
  agreement, never parties to it. (LAN §4¾¾)
- **C19** · Every agreement exists in three registers: plain language, legal,
  and machine-readable. (LAN §4¾¾)
- **C20** · Nothing a participant expresses enters the corpus or leaves the
  room without their recorded consent; a violated consent makes the result not
  exist, whatever the score says. (LAN §4¾ + this instance's hard constraint)
- **C21** · Verifiable trust communities measure fidelity from *outside* the
  agreement — they are the witness from beyond the system. (LAN §4¾¾)

## E · The naming lesson

- **C22** · Naming outsourced to venue infrastructure is a load-bearing
  dependency you discover missing only when standing in a strange room.
  (LAN §3)
- **C23** · Name-keyed routing turns a soft naming failure into a hard one:
  when only one literal string is served, the bare name and the raw address
  both die. (LAN §1–§3, the 502 trap)
- **C24** · Each shared model carries ONE good name of its own; whoever holds
  it names it; there is no canonical string, and everything else is a path
  under the one name. (LAN §4½)
- **C25** · Resolution must be deterministic and identical for every client:
  carry your own naming layer; never depend on venue mDNS. (LAN §5½)
- **C26** · An intermittent name is more dangerous than a dead one — it passes
  every test at home and fails in front of the room. Never trust a warm
  browser; test from a clean resolver, on more than one client. (LAN §1, §5½)
- **C27** · Shared names must never use `.localhost`: browsers force that TLD
  to loopback and ignore the hosts file (RFC 6761). (LAN §5¾, §6)
- **C28** · The front door behind the chosen name must serve a catch-all, so a
  near-miss degrades soft instead of hard. (LAN §4½, §6)

## F · The proverb (Gödel-deepened)

- **C29** · Any agreement complete enough to need no one outside it has either
  stopped listening or begun to lie. (LAN §3)
- **C30** · A trust that certifies itself is the one you cannot trust.
  (LAN §3, lintel form)
- **C31** · Incompleteness is the permanent, healthy condition of a living
  system; truth a system cannot prove about itself must be witnessed from
  outside. So build incomplete, on purpose. (LAN §3)
