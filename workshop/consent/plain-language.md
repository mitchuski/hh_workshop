# Consent — plain language register

*One agreement, three registers (C19) — plus a fourth that is two at once:
this human-readable page, the legal stub, the machine-readable JSON, and the
**Lexon law** (`lexon/consent_agreement.lex`), a controlled-natural-language
text a lawyer can read that a machine also parses — mechanically checked
(`node lexon/check.mjs`) so the law and the code stay one voice. If any
register disagrees with another, we stop and fix it — none of them silently
wins.*

## What you are agreeing to (and what you aren't)

**You propose; we accept.** Under this workshop's standard (IEEE 7012 /
MyTerms), *you* are the one offering terms — the workshop accepts them,
counter-offers once, or declines. Not the other way around (C17). The
agreement is between **you and the workshop-holder** only. The mage, the
harness, and every agent in the room are furniture around the agreement, not
parties to it (C18).

**What "corpus" scope means.** The local machine (the mage) may read your
consented expressions — your drafts, poems, papers, transcribed audio notes —
so it can reflect the room back. It is air-gapped: nothing it reads leaves
the room (C10).

**What "export" scope means.** The named piece may travel — into the bound
books and/or the federation wiki. Nothing travels without this scope.

**You sign per piece, not per person.** One ledger row per expression
(corpus/CONSENT_LEDGER.md), signed by you. No blanket capture; a group
recording only enters trimmed to the voices that consented.

**You can leave.** Withdrawal = your file is deleted, your ledger row struck
(visibly), and the machine's next context pack simply no longer contains you.
Provenance includes the right to leave (INGEST.md).

**Nobody probes you.** You *offer* your device's address to join the room's
network; nothing scans you (C16).

## What the workshop promises back

- The machine never grades you; a blind gate grades renderings, and humans
  hold the doors (FACILITATOR.md rule 4).
- Your name enters the steward chain when you hold the mage's week — that
  record is yours to keep and to verify, forever, offline.
