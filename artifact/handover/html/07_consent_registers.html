The Consent Architecture

Consent — plain language register

One agreement, three registers (C19) — plus a fourth that is two at once: this human-readable page, the legal stub, the machine-readable JSON, and the Lexon law (lexon/consent_agreement.lex), a controlled-natural-language text a lawyer can read that a machine also parses — mechanically checked (node lexon/check.mjs) so the law and the code stay one voice. If any register disagrees with another, we stop and fix it — none of them silently wins.

What you are agreeing to (and what you aren't)

You propose; we accept. Under this workshop's standard (IEEE 7012 / MyTerms), you are the one offering terms — the workshop accepts them, counter-offers once, or declines. Not the other way around (C17). The agreement is between you and the workshop-holder only. The mage, the harness, and every agent in the room are furniture around the agreement, not parties to it (C18).

What "corpus" scope means. The local machine (the mage) may read your consented expressions — your drafts, poems, papers, transcribed audio notes — so it can reflect the room back. It is air-gapped: nothing it reads leaves the room (C10).

What "export" scope means. The named piece may travel — into the bound books and/or the federation wiki. Nothing travels without this scope.

You sign per piece, not per person. One ledger row per expression (corpus/CONSENT_LEDGER.md), signed by you. No blanket capture; a group recording only enters trimmed to the voices that consented.

You can leave. Withdrawal = your file is deleted, your ledger row struck (visibly), and the machine's next context pack simply no longer contains you. Provenance includes the right to leave (INGEST.md).

Nobody probes you. You offer your device's address to join the room's network; nothing scans you (C16).

What the workshop promises back

  • The machine never grades you; a blind gate grades renderings, and humans hold the doors (FACILITATOR.md rule 4).
  • Your name enters the steward chain when you hold the mage's week — that record is yours to keep and to verify, forever, offline.

Consent — legal register (STUB · requires counsel review before first cohort)

Status: DRAFT STUB. This register must be reviewed by qualified counsel (and, for a university deployment, the institution's ethics board) before any participant signs. It exists so the three-register structure (C19) is complete from day one; it is not yet legal advice or a binding instrument.

  1. Parties. This Agreement is bilateral between the individual participant ("First Person") and the workshop-holder ("Second Party"). Software agents, devices, and trust communities are expressly not parties (IEEE Std 7012-2025 posture).
  2. Offer. Terms are proposed by the First Person from the workshop's standard registers; the Second Party may accept, make one counter-offer, or decline.
  3. Licence, per expression. For each expression listed against the First Person's name in the Consent Ledger, the First Person grants the Second Party a revocable, non-exclusive licence limited to the recorded scope: (a) corpus — local machine-reading/grounding on air-gapped hardware within the workshop; (b) export — inclusion in the workshop's bound artefacts and/or federated wiki publication. No other use is licensed.
  4. Revocation. The First Person may revoke per expression at any time; the Second Party shall delete the corpus copy, strike the ledger entry, and rebuild the machine context without it within [N] days. Copies already in printed artefacts or federated forks are governed by [to be drafted].
  5. No automated assessment of the person. Machine outputs are not used as the assessment of record of the First Person.
  6. Records. The Consent Ledger and steward log constitute the record of licences and processing; both are inspectable by the First Person.
  7. Governing law / institution-specific terms. [To be completed with counsel / the partner institution.]
  8. Controlled-language rendering. The structural content of clauses 2–4 is also rendered as a Lexon controlled-natural-language contract (lexon/consent_agreement.lex), mechanically verified for internal coherence (node lexon/check.mjs), including the structural absence of any transfer route to the Second Party. In case of conflict between registers, the parties' recorded intent governs and all registers are corrected together.

lexon/ — the workshop's law, compiling

The workshop's legal-shaped invariants written in Lexon — controlled natural language a lawyer can read AND a machine can parse — and held green by the same gate the lexon_pvm harness uses. This is the fourth face of the consent layer: where workshop/consent/ carries plain-language, legal, and machine-JSON registers as three files, a Lexon law is one text that is both the legal prose and the parseable structure — the babblefish thesis applied to law itself.

What "compiling" means here (the regime, honestly)

There is no public Lexon compiler for this platform (the reference compiler is a macOS-only alpha, source unpublished). This lane runs the spec-checker regime vendored from lexon_pvm: a hand-written validator of an attested subset of Lexon (every grammar production is backed by a lexon.org golden fixture). "Compiles" = passes:

  1. the base gate (tools/lexon_check.mjs) — parse into subject-verb- object triples · noun binding (definitions immutable) · round-trip (triples re-serialize to canonical sentences that re-parse identically) · promise typing (every action edge typed promise/commitment, polarity +);
  2. every relation claim (tools/relation_check.mjs) — the structural fact each law exists to state, verified on the clause graph with a falsifiability twin: a minimally mutated contract must still gate-pass yet fail the claim, or the claim proves nothing. Negative expectations (claims that must come back ABSENT) prove the gate can refuse.

Run it: node lexon/check.mjs — also wired into node tools/check.mjs.

The laws, verbatim (.lex sources)

consent_agreement.lex

LEX Workshop Consent.

"First Person" is a person.
"Workshop Holder" is a person.
"Federation" is a person.
"Expression" is a data.
"Agreement" is a text.
"Terms" is a text.
"Proffered" is a binary.
"Accepted" is a binary.
"Corpus Scope" is a binary.
"Export Scope" is a binary.
"Revoked" is a binary.

The First Person fixes the Terms, fixes the Agreement, and appoints the Workshop Holder.

CLAUSE: Proffer.
The First Person may declare Proffered.

CLAUSE: Accept.
The Workshop Holder may, if Proffered is declared: declare Accepted.

CLAUSE: Standing.
"Consented" is defined as: Proffered is declared and Accepted is declared.

CLAUSE: Scope Corpus.
The First Person may declare Corpus Scope.

CLAUSE: Scope Export.
The First Person may declare Export Scope.

CLAUSE: Admit.
The Workshop Holder may, if this Agreement is Consented: register the Expression.

CLAUSE: Export.
The Workshop Holder may, if Export Scope is declared: send the Expression to the Federation.

CLAUSE: Revoke.
The First Person may declare Revoked.

CLAUSE: Withdraw.
The Workshop Holder must, if Revoked is declared: return the Expression to the First Person.

CLAUSE: Close.
The First Person may, if Revoked is declared: terminate this contract.

The First Person appoints the Steward, and fixes the Fold.

The Steward may, if Consented is declared: register the Expression.

CLAUSE: Close.
