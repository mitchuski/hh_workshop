# The Second Fold

Tomas had laughed, in week four, when the resolver saved the room. In week
five the flight-case key was in his hand and nobody was laughing, least of
all him.

"It's simple," Ilse said, passing it over. "You hold the training. You
don't own the machine. Nobody owns the machine — we all hold it, the way
you hold a ladder someone else is standing on."

The week taught him the shape of it. On Tuesday, Priya sent him her voice
note — sent it, offered it; the rule was carved over the roster page:
*nothing is taken, everything is offered, nobody is scanned.* He ran it
through the transcriber and read the candidate back to her. "That's what I
said," she grinned, "but tidier." Then she signed the line in the ledger
herself — her hand, not his, because consent was the one signature a
steward could never proxy — and only then did the file cross into the
corpus. He had tried, on Monday, to fold in a stray paragraph from a
group chat that nobody had signed for. The packer refused him flatly, and
printed the rule it refused him by. He'd felt oddly grateful.

On Friday he ran the fold in front of everyone. The pack rebuilt; the hash
came up on the shared screen and he read it aloud, all sixty-four
characters, like a strange grace before a meal. "This is what the mage
eats," he said. "Your words. It answers like the room because it is made
of the room." Deeper rewiring — actually changing the weights — stayed a
locked drawer for now; the good way was the machine simply reading them,
live, every session. Ship the good, Ilse always said. Grow into the
perfect.

What surprised him was the door. Marta, who had barely spoken in weeks one
through three, stood up on Friday and read her poem to the machine — and
the machine, fed on four weeks of the room, answered her in an image she
herself had written in week two and forgotten. She went pale, then
delighted. She understood something out loud, and the understanding was
itself her key: the roster showed her a rung higher before she sat down,
no password, no permission slip, just the words she'd proved she could
say. And her poem, of course, was already in the pack for next week. Key
in the lock, food in the bowl, same words twice.

Understand, express, feed the mirror, hear the room answer, go deeper,
again. Tomas signed the log, read his own entry aloud, and handed the key
to Marta.

"Don't drop it," he said, meaning the case.

"Don't worry," she said, meaning the loop.
